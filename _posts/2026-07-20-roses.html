anxiety

{% if page.date %}Olivia Guest, {{ page.date | date: "%e %B %Y" }}{% endif %}

Anxiety is usually different to stress only insofar as it is persistent and remains after the stressor is not present. But AI is apparently ever present, so who knows what I feel. I struggle to really comprehend how unwell I am because of the contemporary unfolding with respect to the erosion of what feels like everything due to AI.

According to what I have learned living in Western Europe for such a long part of my life (roughly from 18-years-old onwards), I express my feelings just fine when I need to. So while in the UK nobody really cared that my stiff upper lip also switches places with me expressing my feelings when I want to, in the Netherlands my ability to express myself, cry in public, or even just have facial expressions, is fetishised.

This has caused me quite a lot of introspection — and given rise to misunderstandings even in feminist spaces, especially in feminist spaces, because I feel so othered for being able to share. But also because I bristle at how within academia the natural tendency here is to outsource all emotional processing from the seniors towards the juniors, or terrifyingly still to students. Yes, it's good to cry. No, it's abusive to expect emotional management from the people you mentor, supervise, or teach. So ironically, witnessing how emotions, superficially presented as positive to express, become a new method of extraction from our more vulnerable colleagues or from our students. So, yes, now I am finally myself scared to share.

can you really teach this?
