Anxiety is usually different to stress only insofar as it is persistent and remains after the stressor is not present. But AI is apparently ever present, so who knows what I feel. I struggle to really comprehend how unwell I am because of the contemporary unfolding with respect to the erosion of what feels like everything due to AI. I'm going to lay out some thoughts with the hope maybe this is helpful to others, healing for myself, and possibly with more processing a way to help us all out of this mess.

should I even share this?

According to what I have learned living in Western Europe for such a long part of my life (roughly from 18-years-old onwards), I express my feelings just fine when I need to. So while in the UK nobody really cared that my stiff upper lip also switches places with me expressing my feelings when I want to, in the Netherlands my ability to express myself, cry in public, or even just have facial expressions, is fetishised.

This has caused me quite a lot of introspection — and given rise to misunderstandings even in feminist spaces, especially in feminist spaces, because I feel so othered for being able to share. I bristle at how within academia here the natural tendency is to outsource emotional processing from the seniors to the juniors and even more terrifyingly to students. Yes, it's good to cry. It's great to express negative and positive emotions in healthy and appropriate ways. But, and I cannot stress this enough, it's abusive to expect emotional management from the people we mentor, supervise, or teach.

Emotions in my new culture, superficially presented as positive to express, are a method of extraction from our more vulnerable colleagues or from our students. The concepts of emotional labour, affective labour, and emotion work are helpful here: simply put, ways of forcing people, usually women, to suppress their own emotional state and act with the goal of making others feel good. In my context, I witness the more powerful, more Germanic, more promoted academics extract emotional labour from those around them. These people jump from no processing of their feelings to vomiting them out on students, for students to mop up, for students to be harmed by the toxicity of the unprocessed nonsense.

So, yes, now I am finally myself scared to share. Where am I sending these feelings? And will the receiver feel compelled to care about mine over theirs? From the ADHD choir of voices, or better put the tornado of thoughts in my head emerges the correction: Nah, just spill it. A website is not a classroom. Your readers are not structurally under your thumb. Do not let emotionally stunted people who cannot manage their own emotions — or their own tendency to extract from others, to extract from others under the guise of emotional maturity even! — get to you. You have processed the feelings a lot. They might even be useful for some. Just spill it.
